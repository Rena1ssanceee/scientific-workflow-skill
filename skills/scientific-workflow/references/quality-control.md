# Final Quality Control

Run these checks before delivery.

## 1. Data consistency

- Do all participant, sample, timepoint, and library counts reconcile?
- Have overlapping participants been deduplicated?
- Is the independent unit correctly defined?
- Are paired and repeated observations modeled as such?
- Is the chronology correct in every section and figure?
- Is group-label provenance stated consistently?
- Are theoretical and analyzable sample counts distinguished?
- Is leakage prevented in training and validation splits?

## 2. Literature consistency

- Does every important citation directly support the associated statement?
- Are titles, years, DOI/PMID, journals, and study designs correct?
- Are 16S, shotgun metagenomics, metatranscriptomics, imaging, and other data types accurately distinguished?
- Are cross-sectional studies not described as longitudinal?
- Are null or conflicting results represented fairly?
- Are user-specified papers included and interpreted correctly?
- Was the search scope frozen before retrieval?
- Were multiple complementary sources and query families used?
- Were synonyms, data types, methods, analogous structures, public datasets, key authors, and competing teams searched as relevant?
- Were backward and forward citation searches completed for key papers?
- Does the master list contain all eligible or plausibly eligible records within scope rather than only representative or high-priority papers?
- Were deduplication, version relationships, search counts, and inaccessible sources documented?
- Was a second-pass search and coverage audit completed?
- Has the search been updated to the final lock date when necessary?

## 3. Scientific inference

- Are association and causation clearly separated?
- Are activity, viability, colonization, infection, mechanism, and prediction used correctly?
- Are exploratory subgroup results labeled as exploratory?
- Are small groups protected from overfitted complex models?
- Are network associations not treated as experimentally confirmed interactions?
- Are enrichment results not written as proven pathways or mechanisms?

## 4. Plan coherence

- Do the title, main question, objectives, analyses, figures, and conclusion match?
- Does every analysis answer a stated objective?
- Does the plan use the major data assets without artificial method stacking?
- Are user restrictions respected throughout?
- Is there one primary storyline?
- Is public-data validation genuinely comparable and clearly scoped?

## 5. Report consistency

- Is the overall conclusion identical in substance to the final-plan and conclusion sections?
- Are terms and abbreviations defined once and used consistently?
- Are timepoints named accurately, for example “post-treatment stable state” rather than “baseline” when appropriate?
- Are figure numbers and analysis modules aligned?
- Are limitations placed near the claims they constrain?
- Is the final report free of unsupported “first”, “novel”, “mechanistic”, or “clinical utility” claims?

## 6. Deliverable checks

- Are requested Word, PDF, spreadsheet, or reference-manager files present?
- Do generated files open correctly?
- Are filenames informative and versioned?
- Are download links provided?
- Is the evidence matrix traceable to the report references?
