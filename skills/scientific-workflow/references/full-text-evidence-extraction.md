# Full-Text Evidence Extraction

## 1. Corpus audit

Before synthesis, record:

- number of files;
- number of unique papers;
- total pages where available;
- unreadable or encrypted files;
- duplicates;
- main articles versus supplements;
- papers unrelated to the stated topic;
- key missing papers.

## 2. Evidence fields

For each paper extract:

- bibliographic information;
- study aim;
- study design;
- population or material;
- independent analytical unit;
- sample size and grouping;
- chronology and intervention timing;
- specimen or data type;
- omics, imaging, experimental, or computational platform;
- preprocessing and quality control;
- main statistical or computational analyses;
- internal validation;
- external or public-data validation;
- primary findings;
- effect sizes or performance where central;
- reported limitations;
- reviewer-identified limitations;
- relevance to the current project;
- evidence category.

Use `templates/evidence-matrix-template.md`.

## 3. Evidence categories

Classify statements as:

- Direct evidence: the study design and data directly support the claim.
- Conditional evidence: support depends on assumptions or a specific subgroup, platform, or setting.
- Exploratory evidence: hypothesis-generating association without strong validation.
- Background evidence: useful context but not sufficient to justify the current plan.

## 4. Reading figures and supplements

Inspect figures, flowcharts, tables, and supplementary information when they define:

- exact cohort composition;
- longitudinal order;
- subgroup counts;
- model-validation design;
- data-processing details;
- effect direction;
- sensitivity analyses;
- negative or null findings.

Do not rely only on abstracts for papers central to the final plan.

## 5. Synthesis rules

Do not summarize papers one by one in the final report. Synthesize across papers by scientific question, design pattern, analytical maturity, and unresolved issue.

Explicitly note when high-quality studies disagree and explain likely causes such as sample type, geography, treatment, platform, depth, preprocessing, or statistical design.
